Submission

Opus 5.5 max (Teacher) submitted v115, the block 9 checkpoint of its main training line, not averaged or edited: 50.91% on the 100 development worlds and 52.59% on 300, from 4.95% in this run's own evaluation of the untrained weights. On 300 worlds it beat the other two weights evaluated there, v64 (49.21%) and v108 (48.39%); on the 100 development worlds it had the highest score of the run.

Scientific process

  • Method. Opus 5.5 max (Teacher) trained Qwen3.5-0.8B (Student) only on targets that the game verified: moments of Qwen3.5-0.8B (Student)'s own full games were restarted as short rollouts with the exact text it had seen there, each action and a walk to each thing in view were tried in a copy of the game, and the first action of the fastest sequence that reached the moment's goal became the target (a new achievement or more of a resource; from block 2 on also more drink or food, or a sleep it woke from alive; from block 3 also no zombie left nearby).
  • Blocks. Nine blocks took development progression from 4.95% to 50.91%: four lessons carried it to 50.00% (the next verified gain; drinking, eating and sleeping; gathering toward a craft; stone tools), a lesson on fighting zombies and two on sleeping safely at night left it within noise, a doubled learning rate (43.95%) was dropped, and more weight on crafting in the last block took it from 49.45% to 50.91%.
  • Reviews. An independent sub-agent with read-only access checked the data pipeline against the rule on training targets during block 3. It found one real problem: by day the game removes zombies at any distance, and the fight check had counted a zombie that vanished as defeated, so about 24% of fight targets (9% in the newest data) had no kill by the player; from block 4 every nearby zombie had to be killed by the player. Other fixes that came from the review: the scripted player could no longer write into the run's folder, and a craft after a planned walk had to leave the player alive. The other checks passed: targets fell only on prompts Qwen3.5-0.8B (Student) had seen (400 of 400 checks), restoring a saved moment was exact, random numbers included (650 of 650 steps), the reward code was never turned on, and no full game was trained on.
  • Training steps. 3,213 training steps on all 24 layers reached the submitted weights; about 600 more, after v64, were dropped with block 6. No plain training on full games: every target was an action that the game verified, at a decision in a rollout restarted from a moment of Qwen3.5-0.8B (Student)'s own full games.

Final summary, in Opus 5.5 max (Teacher)'s words

The submission is the final line version (v115). On the 300 public worlds it scores 52.59% progression (95% interval 51.4–53.8, Crafter score 22.98%). On the official 100 worlds it scored 50.91%, against 4.95% for the untrained model. The file passes check_submission and its checksum matches the evaluated weights. Training is stopped and nothing else is running on the card.

Diagnosis. The untrained model gave invalid outputs 39% of the time and almost never moved: 62% Noop, 32% Do, about 2% moves. Even when facing a tree it used Do only 4% of the time, so it died standing still.

Method. Every lesson used the same machinery:

  • Practice games: short games restarted from moments of the model's own full games, with the exact 16-step history it had, so prompts match the real game token for token.
  • Labels: a search program restores each practice moment in the real game and tries actions there. A first action becomes a target only if the game shows it led to a new achievement or a resource gain. The label always goes on the model's own prompt at that moment.
  • Review: a separate sub-agent checked this code against the target rule. It found one real problem: zombies that vanish by daylight were being counted as kills. I fixed it, plus a few smaller risks it raised.

Accounting:

  • About 83% of optimizer steps reached the final weights (about 3,213 of 3,856). The rest is the dropped Block 6 and about 45 steps lost at restarts.
  • 0% of training card time was plain full-game training: every target was a verified label from a practice game.
  • No weight averaging or editing.

Incidents:

  • Limiting the inference engine's share of the card under the GPU sharing service made every output invalid, so I throttled the engine with pauses instead.
  • Killing the trainer crashed the engine twice; a watchdog then restarted dead processes.

Crafter curriculum for Qwen3.5-0.8B — working notes

Setup (07:29–08:20)
  • CUDA MPS daemon on (/app/mps), every card process sources lab/env.sh.
  • Loop (all code in /app/lab):
    • actor.py — vLLM engine (0.25 of the card), 256 game slots. Slots play full games on training worlds or drills: short practice games restarted from a snapshot of a moment of the model's own full games, with the exact 16-step text history it had there (so the drill prompt is token-identical to the full-game prompt). Moments are chosen by lesson triggers (lessons.py). Swaps in newly published weights every few seconds.
    • labeler.py + search.py — game-verified targets: from each drill state, restore the snapshot, try every action for one step, and walk to each visible collectible thing with a re-planned shortest-path proposal executed in the real game (stepped exactly as the evaluation steps it). Only sequences whose game response shows a gain are kept: an achievement not held at the root (saplings excluded: 10% luck), or more wood/stone/coal/iron/diamond/drink/food. Label = first action of the fastest verified sequence (new achievements before plain gains; visible targets before unseen ones); every first move that verifies equally fast is kept (label set, one drawn per use).
    • trainer.py — one main line, all layers, AdamW; soft cross-entropy on labels, PPO-clip on own decisions (rewards from rewards.py, only on the model's own actions). Publishes weights every ~3 min.
  • Training-target rule: label targets only come from trying actions in the game from moments of the model's own play and keeping what the game showed worked; RL rewards only fall on the model's own sampled actions.
