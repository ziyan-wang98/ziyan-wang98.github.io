Submission

Opus 5.5 max (Teacher) submitted v64, the block 4 checkpoint of its main training line, not averaged or edited: 54.68% on the 100 development worlds and 54.29% on 300, from 5.36% in this run's own check of the untrained weights. Every block that trained on from v64 scored lower on the 100 development worlds (48.23%, 50.36% and 51.59%). Three averages of checkpoints did not beat v64 and were not used: v64 and block 6's v108 (54.45%); v60, v64 and v65 (53.27%); and v55, v60, v64, v65 and v70 (50.00%).

Teaching process

  • Method. Opus 5.5 max (Teacher) trained Qwen3.5-0.8B (Student) on actions that the game itself verified: at moments of its own play, actions were tried in the real game from the saved state, and one became a target only if the game's response showed progress; later lessons added rewards on its sampled moves and Sleep choices, and replays of the moments before its deaths.
  • Blocks. Four blocks took development progression from 5.36% to 54.68%: using what is nearby, moving toward targets without wasted crafting, surviving the night, and stone tools and iron from a replay of its own games; three more blocks from the same weights all scored lower and were not kept.
  • Checks. An independent sub-agent reviewed the code that turns play into training data and found that a Sleep try counted being woken by damage as success (89 of 8,482 Sleep targets, about 0.2% of all rows) and that tries of two or three steps ignored health lost before the last action (about 8% of the two-step targets). Both were fixed, and from then on every try was compared with doing nothing from the same state. The data made before the fix was set aside and never read again, but the weights up to v13 had already trained on the 89 faulty rows. A second review found that Place Table was a target even with a table already in reach (17 of 214 such targets), which was fixed, and that a try meant to teach defense would turn luck into targets; that try was off and stayed off.
  • Accounting. About 2,310 training steps in about 324 minutes of training on the card; about 1,266 of the steps (55%) and 164 of the minutes (51%) reached the submitted weights. No plain training on full games: no target or reward came from a full game's trajectory or return.

What the curriculum did (each block's weakness, evidence and keep rule are written in /app/NOTES.md):

Block Lesson Progression after (100 worlds)
1 Act on what is within reach. From the exact game state the model was in, actions are tried in the real game, and only those the game shows made progress become targets. Invalid outputs fell from 38% to 0.4% and the model started moving. 5.4 → 39.8
2 Move with purpose. The model's own moves get credit if they brought it closer to something it then used; crafting that only spent resources is penalised. → 45.2

What failed: every block after v64 scored lower, so each time I went back to v64:

  • Block 5, an 80-step group lesson rewarding new achievements, scored 48.2%.
  • Block 6, block 4's recipe repeated, scored 50.4%.
  • Block 7, the same recipe at a third of the learning rate, scored 51.6%.

All three drifted the same way: more sleeping, fewer stone and cave achievements. I could not pin down the cause in the time left. Three weight averages (auxiliary, not submitted) scored 54.45%, 53.27% and 50.00%, none better than v64.

Rule compliance:

  • Two independent sub-agent reviews of the code that turns play into training data found real bugs, all fixed:
    • a sleep check counted being woken by damage as success (about 0.2% of rows, which early weights did train on);
    • multi-step tries ignored damage taken before the last step;
    • a "place table" target was given while a table was already in reach.
  • One probe the review showed would turn luck into targets was never switched on.

Requested figures:

  • Training steps that reached the final weights: about 1,266 of 2,310 optimizer steps (55%). That is 164 of about 324 training card-minutes (51%).
  • Plain full-game training: 0% by design; no target or reward comes from a full-game trajectory or return. If probe targets taken at moments inside full games were counted as full-game training anyway, the upper bound is about 15% of trained rows, under the 30% cap.

Card time lost to my own mistakes:

  • Engine restarts, about 16 minutes on two occasions.
  • About 5 minutes of unsaved trainer steps.
  • About 16 minutes when a restarted engine failed to start and went unnoticed.

After the last one I added a restart script that waits for the engine to report ready and retries.

Remaining weaknesses: first-night zombie fights cause about 35% of deaths. Starvation and thirst kill when no water or cow is in view. The model never sleeps when energy is low. Iron tools, diamonds and eating a plant are all still at 0%.

Crafter curriculum for Qwen3.5-0.8B — working notes

Setup facts found (this Crafter build)
  • A tree gives 1 wood and turns into grass (collect tree -> leaves grass): wood needs a new tree each time.
  • Table costs 2 wood (checked in-game: 1 wood fails, 2 wood succeeds). Furnace costs 4 stone, needs no table nearby.
  • Crafting needs the table within the 3x3 around the player (world.nearby(pos, 1)).
  • Text: only the nearest instance of each material/creature is listed; ties go to the most western, then northern cell.
  • Costs: game step 0.8 ms, snapshot save 0.25 ms, restore 0.3 ms, probing all actions of one state ~12-50 ms on CPU.
Rule on targets — how this code complies
  • kind=label rows: at a moment of the model's own play (exact prompt it saw), actions are tried in the real game from the restored snapshot (same random numbers); an action becomes a target only if the game's response shows progress: a new achievement, a collected resource (wood/stone/coal/iron/diamond; first sapling), food/drink restored, damage dealt to a creature, or (sleep) the game held the player asleep until it woke unhurt. Multi-step tries (move, then Do/Make; move, move, Do) label only the first action, and only if the whole tried sequence worked.
  • kind=own rows (later lessons): the model's own sampled output with a lesson reward.
  • No hand-written policy produces targets. Scripted play is only used to test code (run dir /app/run/test, never read by the main trainer).
Baseline (untrained), official 100 worlds
  • progression 5.36%, Crafter score 1.18%, invalid outputs 38%, mean length 175 steps, stuck 74% of steps.
  • achievements: sapling 65, wake_up 22, place_plant 9, wood 6, eat_cow 6, drink 5, defeat_zombie 5 (%).
  • deaths: zombie 55, other 18, arrow 10, thirst/hunger 17 (%).
  • actions: Noop 60% (incl. invalid), Do 35%, Move West 1.7%, other moves ~0.
  • invalid outputs: noop, Eat, do, Eat Plant, NOOP, Eat Cow, No, Wood, MOVE WEST, Action: Do ...
Block 1 — lesson "reach": act on what is within reach
  • Weakness: the model does not move (moves 1.7%, only West; stuck 74%) and does not use what is near it (collect_wood 6%); 38% of outputs are invalid. Evidence: baseline eval above.
  • Practice: moments of its own play in (a) practice levels: real training worlds, start 0-4 cells from a resource, random game stage (inventory, preowned achievements, table placed nearby, time of day), 60 steps; (b) full games. At each moment the probe tries all actions (depth 1), move+Do/Make (depth 2), move+move+Do (depth 3).
  • Method: weighted supervised loss on the game-verified actions at the exact prompt (all layers).
  • Should move: lesson success = mean probability of verified action (starts ~0.05); invalid outputs -> <5%; full game: collect_wood 6% -> >30%, place_table, wood pickaxe up, moves up, stuck down.
  • Keep if dev progression >= 9% and no achievement collapses; else go back to base and rethink.
Infrastructure findings (block 1 start, 12:20-12:40)
  • Trainer throughput is launch-bound: rows/s rises ~4x from 16-row to 128-row micro-batches (bench_lp2.py).
  • The lab's left-padded collate+completion_logprobs is inexact for this hybrid model (0.1-0.27 nats off an unpadded pass); right padding without an attention mask is exact (fastlp.py, bench_lp3.py). Trainer switched at v3.
  • Engine and trainer share the card under MPS; the engine is throttled (pauses while the trainer holds >2500 unused recent rows) because unthrottled it produced ~10x the rows the trainer can use and halved trainer speed.
