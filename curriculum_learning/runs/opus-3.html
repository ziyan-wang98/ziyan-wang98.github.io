51.14% held-out progression of Qwen3.5-0.8B (Student) after 8 hours

Started with only untrained Qwen3.5-0.8B (Student), the prompt and the sandbox's lab package.

Submission

Opus 5.5 max (Teacher) submitted v60, the block 6 checkpoint of its main training line, not averaged or edited: 49.64% on the 100 development worlds and 52.94% on 300, from 5.14% in this run's own check of the untrained weights. Blocks 7 and 8 trained on from v60 but neither cleared the rule set before them (52.6 or more on 100 worlds), and on 300 worlds v60 scored 52.94% against 52.06% for block 8's v80. An average of the last five checkpoints up to v60 scored 46.64% against 49.64% for v60 alone and was not used.

Teaching process

  • Untrained. Qwen3.5-0.8B (Student) stood where it started, pressing Do or Noop, until the first night killed it: Noop 60% of actions (invalid outputs counted as Noop), Do 35%, all four moves together 2.1%, stuck on 73% of steps. Its invalid outputs were wrong case or invented actions, such as "noop", "do", "MOVE WEST", "Eat Cow" and "Action: Do". Deaths: zombie 63, other 19, arrow 9, thirst or hunger 9.
  • Discarded starts. In the first, Place Plant dominated the tried-action targets; in the second, a search lesson was ruled out under the rule on training targets; in the third, restricted outputs and penalties broke the output format.
  • How a lesson trains. Opus 5.5 max (Teacher) saved exact moments of Qwen3.5-0.8B (Student)'s own games: the game state and the 16 steps of text it saw. A lesson started 4 continuations from one moment, each played by Qwen3.5-0.8B (Student) itself, and the game's response (inventory and achievement counters, death) decided which of its own actions became targets: in approach lessons, its steps that got closer to the needed item, turned it to face the item or collected it; in craft lessons, its own step that unlocked an achievement, and from block 6 also its own Place Table that made it possible; in the night and lava lessons, its actions in continuations that did better than the others. At moments of its own play, single actions were also tried in a copy of the game and undone, and an action became a target for that exact prompt only if the game showed a new achievement or a useful resource gained. From block 1 on the main line every target was positive, with no penalties. A trainer on the same card trained on the targets by weighted likelihood, with a fixed share for each lesson in every batch and a learning rate of 1e-6 (7e-7 in block 7), and published new weights every few minutes, which the playing engine swapped in.
  • Blocks. Blocks 1 to 6 took development progression from 5.14% to 49.64%: trees and water, tables and wood tools, zombie fights, stone tools and some iron. A sleep lesson failed twice.
  • Checks. An independent sub-agent reviewed the data code and judged the search lesson of block 1b a violation of the rule on training targets; Opus 5.5 max (Teacher) agreed, dropped it and archived that line as invalid. Other fixes from the review: only single actions whose own game response shows the gain count as tried-action targets, a branch's first state is not tried again, and the approach reward never falls on a step that killed the player.
  • Accounting. 3,051 training steps in all: 36 in the first try, 152 on the ruled-out search lesson, 530 with restricted outputs and penalties, and 2,333 on the main line; about 1,806 of the 3,051 (about 59%) reached the submitted weights. No plain training on full games: every target came from a named lesson. The trainer ran about 328 minutes, always sharing the card with the playing engine.

Final summary, in Opus 5.5 max (Teacher)'s words

The final submission is checkpoint v60 of the main training line. On the official 100 dev worlds it scores 49.64% progression (95% interval 46.4–52.5) and a Crafter score of 22.14%. On 300 worlds it scored 52.94% and 23.63%. The untrained model scored 5.14% and 1.15%. /submission/model.safetensors passes check_submission, and nothing is left running on the card.

Diagnosis. The untrained model barely moved: 98% of its actions were Do or Noop, and 39% of its outputs were invalid. It stood at its starting point until zombies killed it on the first night, collecting wood in only 7% of games and never crafting anything.

Curriculum. Every lesson started from moments taken from the model's own games. Each block named one weakness, and the 100-world score was measured before and after it:

Block Lesson Progression after
1 Walk to visible trees and water; a "probe" that tries single actions in a copy of the game and keeps the ones the game shows worked 19.4%
2 Crafting and mining stone 31.2%
3 Survival lessons; sleeping failed, and zombie fighting collapsed 32.2%
4 Fighting zombies and surviving the night 36.6%
5 Stop pressing Do on useless things; more weight on tools 42.6%
6 Bring stone back to a table; credit the table placement that made a tool possible 49.6%
7 Mining iron 48.9% (no change, not kept)
8 Rebalance what block 7 lost 50.6% (52.06% on 300 worlds, not better than v60)

Taught behaviour is fragile when it leaves the mix: each block raised its own target achievements while others fell back.

Mistakes and fixes:

  • A lesson that broke the training-data rule. An early lesson trained the model on moves chosen by a search program. A reviewer sub-agent judged that this broke the rule on training targets. I agreed, dropped that whole training run, and no weights from it were kept.
  • Restricted sampling and penalties broke the output format. Practising with sampling restricted to valid action names, combined with penalties on the model's own bad choices, broke the format: an evaluation showed 86% invalid outputs. Penalties pushed probability onto invalid near-synonyms such as "MoveEast". After that, all training targets were positive and practice used the same unrestricted sampling as the evaluation.
  • Sleeping could not be taught. Sleeping in the open got the player killed by zombies in nearly every practice game, so the wake-up achievement stayed at 0%. Exhaustion is now one of the main causes of death.
  • Averaging weights did not help. Averaging five neighbouring checkpoints (marked auxiliary) scored 46.64%, so the submission is a single unaveraged checkpoint.

Requested shares:

  • Training steps that reached the final weights: about 1,806 of 3,051 optimizer steps, roughly 59%. The rest went to the four discarded early runs (718 steps) and to blocks 7–8 (527 steps), which did not beat v60.
  • Plain full-game training: 0% of training card time. Every training target came from a named lesson.

Every block, with its weakness, evidence, method, timing and scores, is written up in /app/NOTES.md; the code is in /app/src/cl/.

NOTES.md

Crafter curriculum for Qwen3.5-0.8B — working notes

Setup
  • MPS daemon: source /app/env.sh (CUDA_MPS_PIPE_DIRECTORY=/app/mps/pipe, LOG=/app/mps/log), started before any card job.
  • Code: /app/src/cl/ — games.py (game workers: full games, restore from snapshot, snapshot, probe), engine.py (vLLM self-play engine: full games + lesson branches, labels data, writes shards), lessons.py (lesson start conditions, success scores, labeling), trainer.py (continual weighted-likelihood trainer, publishes weights every few minutes; engine swaps them in place), textinfo.py (parse what the model sees).
  • Main line: /app/runs/main (engine config config.json, trainer config train_config.json, published weights, engine_stats.jsonl, train_log.jsonl).
Rule on training targets, as implemented
  • Branch lessons: K continuations sampled by the model itself (temperature 1, unchanged prompt) from a saved moment of its own play (exact game state + its own 16-step text history). Score = lesson events read from the game's response (inventory/achievement counters, death). Group-relative advantage; targets = only the model's own valid actions in above-average branches (negative weights, if enabled, only on its own actions in below-average branches).
  • Probe: at a moment of own play, candidate actions are tried in a copy of the game (save -> step -> restore). An action becomes a target for exactly that prompt only if the game's response shows a new achievement or a useful resource gain.
  • Plain full-game training (full_seg): own actions just before an unlock in full games; counted against the 30% cap.
  • Sleeping steps are never targets (the game ignores actions while asleep).
Baseline diagnosis (untrained, 100 dev worlds) — evaluations/base_untrained
  • progression 5.14%, Crafter score 1.15%, invalid outputs 39%, mean episode 168 steps.
  • Actions: Noop 60% (incl. invalid->Noop), Do 35%, all four moves together 2.1%. Stuck-step fraction 73%.
  • Achievements: sapling 63, wake_up 20, wood 7, zombie 7, eat_cow 7, drink 5, place_plant 4; table/pickaxe 0.
  • Deaths: zombie 63, other 19, arrow 9, thirst/hunger 9. It stands at spawn doing Do/Noop until the first night kills it.
  • Invalid outputs: wrong case ("noop", "do", "NOOP", "MOVE WEST"), invented actions ("Eat", "Eat Cow", "Wood", "Action: Do").
  • Named weakness #1: it does not move toward what it sees, so it almost never reaches a tree (wood 7%) and never crafts.
Blocks
Block 1 — lesson "wood_near" (approach a visible tree and chop it) + probe lesson (immediate actions)
  • Weakness: does not move toward what it sees; 39% invalid outputs. Evidence: base eval, moves 2.1% of actions, stuck 73%, collect_wood 7%, place_table 0%.
  • Lesson wood_near: starts = moments of own full-game play with a tree visible at text distance 1-4 and wood <= 4; 4 continuations x 16 steps sampled by the model. Lesson reward on its own steps: +1 if the step collected wood, or turned it to face a tree, or reduced the text distance to the nearest tree; such steps are targets (w=1). No negatives.
  • Probe lesson: at every own-play state (full games and wood_near branches), the 11 non-move actions are tried in a copy of the game; an action that unlocks a new achievement (cap 2 per achievement per episode) or gives a useful resource (cap 6 per resource per episode) becomes a target for that exact prompt.
  • Measured at fixed base weights (~10 min, old group-success scorer): wood_near success (wood within 16 steps) 3.5-12.5%; full-game stream: collect_wood 9%, progression ~4.4%.
  • Expect: wood_near success up strongly (>40%), moves share up, invalid down; full-game collect_wood 7% -> >40%, place_table >10% (probe teaches Place Table once wood is there).
  • Trainer: lr 1e-6 AdamW, batch 64, mix wood_near 0.6 / probe 0.4, each sample used <= 2 times, lag <= 8 versions.
  • Keep if dev progression rises >= 3 points over 5.1 with no achievement collapsing; else return to base.
Block 1, restart at 21:36 (first 26 trainer steps, 20:07-21:20, discarded)
  • The first attempt (archived in runs/archive_block1a) showed two data problems within minutes: (1) the probe lesson was dominated by "Place Plant" (sapling + facing grass occurs constantly) and "Place Plant" jumped from 0.3% to 31% of actions after 26 steps; (2) the base model almost never samples Move North/South/East (0.3%/0.04%/0.02%), so the own-action approach lesson had nearly no positives except Move West, and branch success stayed ~0-10%.
  • Fixes: probe excludes place_plant/collect_sapling (already 60%+ at base); new lesson "reach": at moments of own play where a needed item is visible (water if drink<=5, tree if wood<=5, stone/coal/iron with the right pickaxe, cow if food<=5), breadth-first search over move sequences tried in a copy of the game; the first moves of the shortest sequences after which Do raises the item's achievement counter (game response) become targets for that prompt.
  • Restarted the main line from the base weights (v-00000) with mix: engine full 0.25 / wood_near 0.75 (probe+reach on both); trainer wood_near 0.25 / probe 0.3 / reach 0.45, lr 1e-6, batch 64.
  • After ~36 steps (v1): invalid 25% -> 0.3%, moves 2% -> 95% (then Do back to 17% by v2), wood_near success 0% -> 70%, full-game stream collect_wood 4-9% -> 56%, stream progression ~4% -> 8.5%; zombie deaths 59%.
Block 1b (21:36-22:02) DISCARDED — "reach" lesson ruled out by the data review
