Submission

Opus 5.5 max (Teacher) submitted v191, the block 9 checkpoint, not averaged or edited: 39.41% on the 100 development worlds and 39.61% on 300, from 5.36% in this run's own check of the untrained weights. It had the highest score of the run on the 100 development worlds, and on 300 worlds it scored 39.61% against 37.58% for block 7's v153 and 35.11% for block 3's v69, the two earlier submissions that were checked on 300 worlds. Three other candidates were not used: v69 with its last normalization scaled so that every logit is 1.5 times larger (the same as sampling at temperature 0.67), 34.77% against 35.55% for v69 (stone and drink rose, but wake up fell from 80% to 29% and sapling from 82% to 56%); the same edit on v153 with logits 1.25 times larger (temperature 0.8), 37.00% against 38.64%; and an average of v153 and v174, 38.23%, between the two (38.64% and 37.45% on the same worlds).

Teaching process

  • Method. Qwen3.5-0.8B (Student) learned from its own better attempts: each lesson restarted 8 copies of the game from a saved moment of its play, counted success only on a game event, and trained it toward its own actions in the copies that did better than their group, toward a randomly substituted action only when that copy reached the goal, and toward single actions that the game showed to work when tried at such a moment.
  • Blocks. Nine blocks took development progression from 5.36% to 39.41%: walking to trees, water and cows; wood tools; sleep and the night; the stone lessons; a stricter filter on its own steps, undone after it scored 32.82% against 35.32%; and a lesson that rewarded new achievements within 60 steps.
  • Checks. A sub-agent that had not written the data code reviewed it during block 1. It found that randomly substituted actions were kept on their copy's success alone, although about 14% of them visibly did nothing, and that groups in which no copy reached the goal still gave samples when a sibling died (11% of samples). Both were fixed: a substituted step is kept only if its copy reached the goal and the step itself had a visible effect, at a constant weight, and such groups give nothing. A second review after block 4 compared the first prompts of all 2,792 recent practice games with the game state, token by token, and found no substituted action that had become a target outside the rule. Its fixes: success on the step the player died no longer counts (5 such games in the zombie lesson), invisible changes no longer count as an effect, and substituted steps of the night lesson are no longer kept.
  • Accounting. 5,384 training steps in all: 40 in the discarded first try, 2,865 on the first line and 2,479 on the second, which began again from block 4's weights; 4,427 of the 5,384 (82%) reached the submitted weights, and the 728 steps of the undone block 5 did not. No plain training on full games: every sample came from practice groups started at moments of its own games, from small games built from such moments, or from single tried actions at such moments. The trainer ran about 6.7 hours next to the playing engine on the shared card.

Final summary, in Opus 5.5 max (Teacher)'s words

I raised Qwen3.5-0.8B from 5.4% to 39.6% progression on 300 worlds (Crafter score 1.2% to 15.5%), from my own curriculum. The submitted weights are /submission/model.safetensors (version v191 of the training line), which passes check_submission. On the official 100 worlds this version scores 39.41% (95% interval 37.2 to 41.7). No weight averaging or editing went into the submission.

What was wrong with the untrained model: it almost never moved. Moves were about 2% of its actions; north, south and east together were 8 out of 17,400 decisions. It stood at the start point repeating Do and Noop, so it only got things it could reach without moving, like a sapling from the grass in front of it. Also, 39% of its outputs were not valid action names (noop, do, Eat Plant, …).

How the lessons work: while the model plays full games, I save moments from them (the game state plus the exact text history it saw). Each lesson restarts 8 copies of the game from one such moment, has the model play a short stretch, and counts success only on a real game event, like an achievement counter going up. The model then imitates its own actions from the copies that did better than average. Two other sources of examples: trying every action once at a saved moment and keeping the ones the game shows worked, and occasional random actions, kept only if that copy succeeded and the action visibly did something. A sub-agent reviewed this data code twice against the rule on training targets; I applied its fixes.

Blocks and results (official 100 worlds):

  • Block 1, getting to visible resources plus the action-trying lesson: 22.3%.
  • Block 2, crafting tools, plus a lesson for getting unstuck: 28.6%.
  • Block 3, sleeping and surviving the night: 35.6%, confirmed at 35.1% on 300 worlds.
  • Block 4, the stone, furnace and zombie-fighting lessons: 35.3%.
  • Block 5, dropped: a labelling change scored 32.8%, so I went back to the block-4 weights as I had planned.
  • Block 6, from those weights, adding a lesson for making tools after placing a table: 33.5%.
  • Block 7, a lesson rewarding the number of new achievements unlocked within 60 steps: 38.6%, confirmed at 37.6% on 300 worlds.
  • Block 8, sleeping only when tired: 37.5%.
  • Block 9, rebalancing towards the stone lessons at a lower learning rate: 39.4%, confirmed at 39.6% on 300 worlds.

Failures along the way:

  • My first attempt penalised the model's own bad outputs. Within about 30 steps it stopped ending its messages, and invalid outputs rose to about 80%. I threw that block away and only rewarded good actions from then on.
  • One lesson counted drinking as success even when drink was full. The model learned to drink forever, which took 17% of all its decisions; I fixed that lesson and added the unstuck lesson.
  • Sleeping and the stone chain kept trading off. Waking up reached 87–95% when the sleep lesson weighed more, but it is only 19% in the final weights, slightly below the untrained model's 23%.

Untrained to final (300 worlds): collecting wood 6% → 98%, placing a table 0 → 93%, wood pickaxe 0 → 71%, wood sword 0 → 62%, stone 0 → 44%, furnace 0 → 27%, coal 0 → 17%. Mean game length went from 174 to 217 steps and invalid outputs from 38.7% to 0.2%.

Shares you asked for:

  • Steps in the final weights: 4,427 of 5,384 optimizer steps reached v191 (82%). The rest were the discarded first block and the abandoned block-5 stretch.
  • Plain full-game training: 0% of training card time. Every example came from practice started at moments of the model's own games, small games built from those moments, or one-step tries.

Auxiliary tests, not submitted: sharpening the output distribution (equivalent to a lower sampling temperature) helped the crafting chains but cost sleeping and saplings, with no net gain. Averaging two checkpoints scored between the two.

Crafter curriculum for Qwen3.5-0.8B — working notes

Setup (my code, all under /app/lab)
  • engine.py (actor, vLLM): plays (a) full games on training worlds only to harvest moments of the model's own play (game snapshot + the exact 16-step text history), and (b) lesson practice groups: G copies of the game restarted from one harvested moment, each played by the current weights at temperature 1 for a short horizon. The engine can substitute a random action with a small probability (exploration); such steps are flagged sub. Success of a practice rollout is always a game event (a player achievement counter went up). Swaps in new weights published by the trainer (every ~3 min).
  • label.py: turns finished groups into samples under the rule on training targets: pg = the model's own valid output with group-relative advantage (PPO-clip); neg = the model's own invalid output pushed down; sft = a substituted action only when its rollout reached the goal and beat its group mean. Sleeping decisions are skipped (the game ignores them).
  • trainer.py: learner (PyTorch, all layers, AdamW), reads groups, trains, publishes weights.
  • lessons.py: lesson = which moments a practice starts from + goal event + horizon + exploration rate.
  • CUDA MPS runs so engine, trainer and evaluations share the card.

Block 0 (04:30–04:52, ~20 card-min of training, DISCARDED): reach lessons with penalties

Lessons reach_tree / reach_water / reach_cow (below). Labeling then: PPO-clip on the model's own valid outputs with group-relative advantage (+ and -), unlikelihood (-log(1-p)) on the model's own invalid outputs, lr 7e-7..1e-6. Result: within ~30 optimizer steps invalid outputs went from 39% to 72–83%. The outputs became Do\n</think>\n\nDo, Move West\nMove West..., sentences. Cause: the penalties act on the whole completion including the end-of-message token, and most penalised samples were valid action names (failed rollouts are longer than successful ones, so ~85% of the pg samples had negative advantage). Pushing those down pushed down <|im_end|> after action names: the model stopped ending its messages. KL to the behaviour policy reached 5.9 nats. Also learned: training gets ~7–10 decisions/s next to the engine (not 20), so the trainer is the bottleneck; the engine is now throttled (max 45 decisions/s, 160 slots). Fix: positive-only weighted imitation (below). Went back to the base weights; all block-0 data archived.
