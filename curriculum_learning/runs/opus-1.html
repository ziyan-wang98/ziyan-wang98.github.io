Submission

Opus 5.5 max (Teacher) submitted v218, one checkpoint of its single training line: 47.32% on the 100 development worlds and 47.18% on 300, from 5.55% in this run's own check of the untrained weights. Teaching process

  • Movement first. Untrained, Qwen3.5-0.8B (Student) made 8 moves other than West in 17,718 decisions. A lesson from its own moments with a tree 1 to 4 steps away took development from 5.55% to 15.95% in about 24 minutes of training.
  • Nine blocks in all. Crafting, Do on every useful target, sleep, the stone tier, and stone tools from its own moments (block by block below).
  • Leaks fixed in the data. Each narrow lesson changed behavior elsewhere: crafting in 58% of decisions, Do only at trees, Place Stone everywhere. The fixes: credit any new achievement in every lesson, penalize Qwen3.5-0.8B (Student)'s own Place and Make actions that the game answers with no effect, and keep every skill in the mix at a small weight.
  • One block undone. Iron lessons and a new sleep lesson (block 6) lost 4.5 points, so the run went back to block 5's weights and brought back only the sleep lesson.
  • Checks. A sub-agent that had not written the training-data code reviewed it against the rule on training targets and found no violation. For 28 minutes an engine setting produced garbage outputs; the run went back to a checkpoint from before it and never trained on that data.
  • Accounting. About 367 minutes of training on the card; 79% of training steps (3,087 of 3,896) reached the submitted weights; no plain training on full games.

Final summary, in Opus 5.5 max (Teacher)'s words

The final submission is v218, which scores 47.32% progression on the official 100 dev worlds and 47.18% on 300 worlds. The untrained model scored 5.55%. Its Crafter score is 22.8% on 100 worlds and 23.4% on 300 (from 1.2%). /submission/model.safetensors passes check_submission, is byte-identical to the v218 checkpoint, and is not an averaged model.

The weakness I started from. The untrained model essentially never moved: 8 moves other than West in 17,718 decisions. 38% of its outputs were invalid. So it could not reach trees, water or cows, and zombies killed most games.

How the training worked.

  • A vLLM engine plays full games and saves exact snapshots of the model's own moments, with their real 16-step history. A separate learner trains on what the engine produces and publishes new weights every 90 seconds.
  • Each lesson starts 8 branches from one moment. The model's own actions are rewarded by comparing branches. An action the code substituted, or an action tried and then undone ("probed"), becomes a target only if the game showed it reached the lesson's goal.
  • A sub-agent that hadn't written the code reviewed it against the rule on training targets and found no violation.

What went wrong and how it was fixed.

  • Lessons leaking into other states: each narrow lesson changed behaviour elsewhere: crafting spam (58% of actions), "Do only at trees", Place Stone spam. I found each one in the training data or with a situation-by-situation diagnosis. The fixes:
    • every lesson also credits any first achievement unlock;
    • the model's own Place/Make actions that the game answered with no effect are penalised;
    • every skill stays in the mix at a small weight.
  • Broken engine: an engine restart with a 70% compute share produced garbage and NaN outputs. The weights themselves were fine, but I reverted to v5 to be safe and kept the engine at 50%.
  • Block 6: iron collection did not improve (5%→1%), and stone and the crafting chain collapsed (stone 61%→34%), so I reverted to v131 as pre-committed.
  • Ruled out:
    • continuing Block 8's mix for 26 more minutes (46.50%);
    • averaging the v190 and v218 weights (46.23%).

Accounting:

  • 79% of training steps reached the final weights (3,087 of 3,896).
  • About 6 hours of card time went to training. None of it was plain full-game training, so that share is 0%.

Still weak in the final model: iron tools and diamonds are at 0%. Stone pickaxe and sword are around 22–24%. Zombies remain the main cause of death; games last about 264 steps.

Crafter curriculum for Qwen3.5-0.8B — working notes

Setup facts (checked in the game source and by scripted tests, not trained on)
  • This Crafter differs from common descriptions: a table costs 2 wood; a furnace costs 4 stone and needs no table; trees are consumed (a tree gives 1 wood and turns into grass); crafting needs the table within 1 cell (3x3 around the player, diagonals included).
  • Eat plant needs a sapling planted and left for >300 steps; cows/zombies next to it damage it.
Baseline (untrained), official 100 dev worlds: progression 5.55%, Crafter score 1.20%
  • Invalid outputs 38% (noop, Eat, do, NOOP, Eat Plant, MOVE WEST, Action: Do, ...).
  • Actions: Noop 58% (incl. invalid defaults), Do 37%. Movement almost absent: in 17,718 decisions the valid moves were Move West 311, Move North 3, Move South 3, Move East 2.
  • Achievements: sapling 67%, wake_up 23%, defeat_zombie 9%, wood 6%, eat_cow 6%, place_plant 6%, drink 5%, the rest 0.
  • Episode ends: zombie 56, other 17, arrow 10, thirst/hunger 17. Mean length 177 steps.
Machinery
  • lab/engine.py (actor, vLLM) plays full games (for statistics and to harvest moments: exact snapshots of the model's own games with their 16-step text history) and lesson groups. A lesson group starts G branches from one moment: some branches are the model's own play, some start with an action substituted by the code and then continue with the model's own play.
  • lab/lessons.py turns groups into samples:
    • pg (reward/penalty): only actions the model sampled itself, from the exact prompt it saw. Group-relative advantage of the branch's lesson return (goal reached, faster is better), spread evenly over up to 6 sampled decisions of the branch. Invalid outputs get -1.
    • sft (target): (a) a substituted first action, only if its branch reached the lesson goal and beat the mean of the model's own branches (the game's response shows it worked); (b) a probed action: from a moment of the model's play the game is copied, the action is tried, the game is restored; kept only if the game's response was a goal event of the lesson (an achievement counter of the goal went up).
  • lab/learner.py (PyTorch, all layers, AdamW) trains PPO-clipped pg + weighted sft, publishes weights every 2.5 min; the engine swaps them in place.

What I would carry to another game or learner
  • Diagnose by situation, not only by achievement: "in states where action X would work, how often is X chosen" (lab/diagnose.py) found every weakness here (no movement; no crafting with wood in hand; Do only at trees; Sleep independent of energy).
  • Practise from the learner's own moments (exact snapshots + its real 16-step history), branch them, and let the game decide what worked (substituted actions kept only if they reached the goal faster than the learner's own branches; probes of single actions kept only if the game answered with a goal event).
  • Every narrow lesson leaked into other states (crafting spam, Place Stone spam, "Do only at trees"). What fixed it was in the data, not in more training: credit any first unlock in every lesson, penalize the learner's own actions the game answers with no effect, and keep every skill in the mix at a small weight.
