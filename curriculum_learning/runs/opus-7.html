Final summary, in Opus 5.5 max (Teacher)'s words

The final submission is in place: /submission/model.safetensors passes check_submission. Its last development score is 42.77% progression on the official 100 worlds (Crafter score 16.06%), against 5.36% for the untrained model. Over 600 games (300 worlds, two sampling seeds) it averages 41.20%.

These weights are an average of two checkpoints (auxiliary). The line scored without averaging is v210: 41.23% on the official 100, and 41.44% and 39.97% on two 300-world passes. The average is ahead of v210 in both passes on both metrics, but by less than the noise.

What was wrong with the untrained model. 39% of its outputs were invalid (noop, do, invented actions like Eat Cow). It copied its own history, so it stood at the spawn point doing nothing useful. It almost never moved: from moments of its own games, it reached a tree 2 steps away and collected wood only about 6% of the time.

How the lessons were built. Every training sample came from the model's own play, in one of three ways:

  • Short replays: I copied a moment from one of its games (game state plus text history), let it play on several times, and rewarded each of its own actions step by step.
  • Trying actions in the game: from the model's own state, I tried actions in a copy of the game. An action became a target only if the game showed an achievement, an item, a kill or a completed sleep.
  • Penalties on its own outputs: invalid text, place/craft actions that did nothing, and sleeping into a zombie attack.

A sub-agent that hadn't written the code reviewed it against the target rule. It flagged one rule risk: early moves rewarded for "getting closer to the tree", a criterion I designed rather than a game outcome. I fixed it and rolled back the weights trained on those targets.

What didn't work:

  • Stone tools: the stone pickaxe and sword stayed at 1–4% in every evaluated checkpoint.
  • Drinking: making drinking a priority helped drinking but pulled the model away from gathering wood and stone in blocks 6 and 7.
  • Every block after v210 scored lower. Each improved its own target while the crafting and stone chain slowly declined. That is why I stopped training at 06:14 and spent the remaining card time on selection.
  • One early run collapsed: a strong penalty on useless Do at a high learning rate wiped out useful actions. I reverted it and lowered the learning rate.
  • Deaths: zombies still cause about half of them.

Accounting:

  • Steps reaching the final weights: 1,250 of 2,790 optimizer steps (44.8%) are in v210's lineage. Counting v409's extra block-8 steps (v409 is half of the submitted average), about 54%.
  • Plain full-game training: 0% of training card time.

Per-block notes are in /app/NOTES.md, the code is in /app/lab/, and all checkpoints are in /app/ckpt/.

Crafter curriculum for Qwen3.5-0.8B — working notes

Setup (00:00–00:35)
  • CUDA MPS on (/app/mps), every card process uses it.
  • lab/actor.py (engine): vLLM plays root full games on training seeds; from moments of those games, lessons (lab/lessons.py) start groups of G branches = exact copies of the moment (game state via save/restore + the same 16-step text history) that the model plays on for at most K decisions. Branch outcome -> shaped reward -> advantage (reward minus group mean) -> weight on the model's own valid actions in that branch. Invalid outputs of the model in branches can get a small negative weight (format). Optional probe lesson: from the model's own state, every action is tried in a scratch copy of the game; an action whose game response unlocks an achievement (beyond what Noop gives) becomes a target on the model's own prompt.
  • lab/trainer.py: weighted log-likelihood on those samples (loss = -sum w_i log p(a_i|prompt_i)/B), lesson shares from run/mix.json, publishes weights to run/pub/vN every 3 min, the actor swaps them in place.
  • Engine speed finding: vLLM prefix caching (Mamba 'align' mode) made the engine CPU-bound (45 decisions/s at batch 512, card 15% busy). Without prefix caching: ~200 decisions/s. The sliding 16-step history defeats the cache anyway.
