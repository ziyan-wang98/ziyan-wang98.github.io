Experience to Knowledge

A model turns its own experience into short, general knowledge, keeps that knowledge in its weights, and uses it. We test this on cd82, a game from ARC-AGI-3, with Qwen3.5 9B. Status on 16 November 2026.

Motivation

People do not remember every move they made. They keep a few short rules, such as "this button undoes that one", and use them again in new places. Over time they find rules that hold more widely.

We want the same for a model. From its experience it writes knowledge as short sentences, learns these sentences into its weights, and uses them when it decides. Then it uses this knowledge to explore, finds more general knowledge, and repeats. This is recursive self improvement (RSI) in the space of knowledge. The goal is knowledge that also works on new tasks.

In symbols. Experience is a set of trajectories τ. Knowledge is a set of short sentences

K = {k1, …, kn},    level ℓ(k) ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3}

where ℓ = 0 is the answer to one instance (a fixed route), 1 is a rule of one game level, 2 is a mechanism across game levels and 3 is a principle across games. After learning K, the model πθ should

  1. recite: say ki when asked, with no K in its prompt;
  2. use: state the right ki when it decides, and act on it;
  3. gain: do better on this task and on new tasks, and raise ℓ over rounds.

How this differs from RL, SFT, self distillation and memory

The question is what is learned, where it is kept, and whether it is short (compact) and holds widely (general).

Related findings on knowledge in weights: a fact can be stored in a model and still not be used unless training shows it in many forms [22, 23]; fine tuning learns new facts best when each fact appears in many paraphrases [24]. Our results below show the same gap for rules: recited, but rarely used.

The picture

Three steps

STEP 1

Summarize and filter

Extract knowledge from trajectories. Check each claim with new experiments. Drop wrong claims and low level ones (answers, routes, coordinates).

On cd82: 474 claims extracted, 391 kept. A 5 step route the model wrote itself reached 100/100 when put in the prompt, and was dropped: its knowledge level is 0.

STEP 2 · NOW

Write it into the weights

First make the model recite the knowledge. Then make it use the knowledge when it decides, with no knowledge in its prompt.

Recite: done (55% of replies). Use: working on it with readers, rounds of training and RL.

STEP 3

Raise the level

Use the learned knowledge to explore, extract more general knowledge, filter it, learn it, and repeat.

Not started. Needs step 2 to work first.

Step 1: how the knowledge is made

Step 1 turns raw play into checked rules, K. One model plays, writes claims and checks them: Qwen3.8 27B, 3 to 12 September 2026, game levels 1 to 4 of cd82. Level 1 is the first game level, the one the rest of this page uses. Levels 5 and 6 are held out of K: the checker could open them only for a correctness check (6 sessions did), never for generality, and code drops any statement about them.

From games to K

1 · PLAY

Record games

100 recorded games per level, up to 300 actions, three tools: look, act, status. 9 of 100 clear level 1; none clears levels 2 to 4.

400 games

2 · SUMMARIZE

Write claims

48 runs of the model per level read a text record of the games. Each run writes up to 3 rules: condition, effect, evidence, and a test that could prove the rule wrong.

474 claims

3 · CORRECT?

Test each claim

For each claim, a new agent session tests every part of it, with code on the recorded games and in new games, with control cases in about half of them.

436 with a record of the tests: 5,495 checks, 14,196 game actions

4 · GENERAL?

Test on other levels

Each test should be run again on two other levels. Most were not.

197 of 436 tested on another level

5 · FILTER

Drop what fails

A filter in plain code, with no model, drops refuted and untested claims, answers (moves, coordinates, level solutions) and anything about levels 5 and 6.

391 kept

6 · REWRITE

Make statements

Each kept claim becomes the checker's corrected statement, cleaned by code; 44 were replaced by hand-checked rewrites made from the claim text alone. K for level 1 is the 198 that hold there, minus 14 that mention the action budget of the harness (our program that runs the game for the model and allows 300 actions).

184 statements

Verdicts on the 436: 110 hold, 65 hold under a condition, 228 partly refuted (the main part held, one or more other parts did not), 15 refuted, 13 untested, and 5 with no overall verdict (4 of them kept, their parts hold). 80 claims were checked twice, and the two verdicts agreed for 44. No person audited the verdicts.

A claim and its experiment

"An ACTION5 immediately after another ACTION5 never pours: it changes at most one ambient cell, or nothing […]". The claim added that any other action brings the pour back, "even one that changed 0 cells".

The checker ran 26 checks and 61 game actions, 42 of them on levels 2 to 4:

  • A repeat never pours: 0 exceptions in 101 recorded pairs and in 17 new tries.
  • A pour needs another action or a new board first: 466 of 466 recorded, 8 of 8 new.
  • Any action that changed nothing brings the pour back: refuted. No pour in 48 of 71 recorded cases and in 8 of 8 new ones.

Verdict: partly refuted. The part that held, held on all four levels, and entered K as "Consecutive ACTION5s never repaint […]".

1. Writing knowledge in without breaking reasoning

The first try, on the 27B in September, was SFT on bare statements: a chat row asks about a rule and the reply states it. The model learned the shape of the row more than the rule. In the game it stated a fact as its reply and stopped. A note from 8 November also records that its math fell, that merging the weights back with the base model brought math and format back but no knowledge use, and that a graft from Qwen3.5 failed. Only that one note records these three; the format break is in the public model cards, and no merged or grafted model was published.

What kept the reasoning, from 18 September, adds to the model's own thinking instead of replacing it. A row is a game turn of the model, unchanged, with one to three sentences of K at the start of its thinking. On the 27B, 502 of 1664 rows got no sentence, and the sentences were 1.2% of trained tokens (69,278 of 5.79M), so their loss weight rose step by step to 32. On the 9B, 17.5% of rows got none, the sentences were about 12% of trained tokens, and a turn was kept only when the 9B's own sample took the recorded act (119 of 303 turns). No merge was used. The model already predicts the rest of the row well, so the loss falls almost only on the sentences and its way of reasoning stays. The 27B's math was not recorded. On the 9B, MATH-500 rose only because fewer replies ran out of tokens; on finished replies it is flat (92.0%, then 91.3%). The cost: after this step 16.5% of its game replies mix in words of other languages (0.8% before), and each later round of training on its own replies raised that further.

2. The model can recite, but recitation is not use

We trained the 9B with knowledge sentences put at the start of its own thinking. It now says the sentences, but almost always in the first paragraph, and then it thinks as before. On unseen states the act it takes has nothing to do with what it recited.

3. Knowledge in the prompt helps a lot

When the same knowledge is in the prompt, both models act much better. When the whole list is written at the start of the 9B's own thinking, it works almost as well as the prompt. So the model can act on knowledge. The hard part is to recall the right sentence at the moment of the decision.

The 27B numbers come from the raw games in the public dataset ziyan98/E2K-cd82-harness-knowledge (7 to 8 September, before the current knowledge set). With the 5 step route in the prompt it was 100/100; routes never enter training, so it is not drawn. The 9B numbers are from one H200, on the same 59 unseen states.

4. How we judge "use"

  1. Words: does the thinking contain a sentence of K (8 or more words, 80% of its 4 word pieces)? Where?
  2. 27B judge: given K but not the outcome, does the act follow from a stated rule of K? Two passes, the second one strict with quotes.
  3. Two blind readers (Claude): a use counts only if the rule decides this act, the aim is the goal or a test with a stated effect, the act matches the decision, and the rule was not just seen in this episode. A training row needs both readers (κ = 0.83); a test number is the mean of the two.
  4. Cause: delete the rule sentence from the thinking, let the model continue from that point, and see whether the act changes.
A flaw we found: 481 of the 692 training states carry, in the prompt, a summary of earlier play written by the agent that recorded the episode, and it states rules such as "ACTION4 undoes ACTION3". Readers see only the thinking, so some "uses" in training data were read from that summary. The 59 test states are early in their episodes and have no summary, so test numbers are clean. From now on we train only on states without a summary.

5. Round 3: the rule now drives the act, but not always the right act

Two blind readers read 354 replies on the unseen states: 2 replies that act per state from each of rounds 1, 2 and 3, mixed together with no labels. Strict use, weighted per state:

round 1: 3.0%,   round 2: 6.1%,   round 3: 16.8%;   round 3 − round 2 = +10.7 points, 95% CI [4.5, 17.5];   κ = 0.76

By the looser standard (the rule decides the act, any aim) it is 3.8%, 8.1% and 25.8%. So after round 3 the model recalls a rule and acts on it in about one reply in six, with nothing in its prompt.

Is the rule really the cause? For 14 strict uses of the round 3 model on unseen states, we removed the rule sentence and sampled 12 new endings with and without it.

Δ = P(same act | rule kept) − P(same act | rule removed) = 0.19,   95% CI [0.05, 0.39]

It was 0.07 [−0.01, 0.18] after round 2 and −0.02 after round 1. So for the first time the stated rule changes what the model does. But with the rule kept, the step made progress in 33% of endings, and with it removed in 45%. The model now uses knowledge, but not always correctly. This is why the RL reward below pays only for knowledge that is used and leads to progress.

RL

What we ran before

  1. September, 27B, another cluster: 32 games per round, 40 steps per game. The knowledge model cleared 1/32, the base 0/96. An earlier claim that knowledge made RL better was withdrawn on 30 September: 32 games vary by 5 to 9 points by chance.
  2. 14 to 15 November, 27B, one H200: outcome only RL for 4 rounds, then 2 rounds with a guided knowledge bonus. On unseen states, outcome only RL raised one situation (right after ACTION5: 0.19 → 0.28 acting right) and nothing else; the guided bonus moved nothing; the knowledge model did not learn faster than a control trained without knowledge.
  3. 15 November, 9B: outcome only RL for 4 generations (progress 2.1% → 5.3%), then RL with a "say and use" bonus for 10 generations (progress 5.7% → about 11%). The bonus was paid 1 time in 4720 test replies: it was outcome RL in practice, and the knowledge was not used.
  4. 15 to 16 November, 9B: three rounds of expert iteration (below). Rewarded replies are the ones where the model states a rule, acts on it and makes progress; the update is SFT on them.

What runs now

Round 3 gives the RL a foothold: the 27B pass 1 calls 32% of the acting replies a use, and the strict pass 3.5%. The reward pays correct use, not recitation:

r = 0.5 · o + 1.0 · 1[strong use] + 0.5 · 1[weak use] − 0.5 · 1[other language words],   o = +1 if the step makes progress, else −1 Ai = ri − meanj∈group rj
  • A use is paid only when the step makes progress, so a wrongly used rule earns nothing.
  • Strong use: both 27B passes and the strict checks agree (the rule stands in the reasoning after the first paragraph, the decision is quoted near the end and matches the act, the rule is a statement of K). Weak use: pass 1 alone.
  • Recitation earns nothing: a rule that only stands in the first paragraph never counts.
  • The model's prompt never contains K; only the judge sees it. The judge never sees the outcome. We train only on the 211 states without a summary of earlier play.
  • A reply loses 0.5 when its thinking mixes in words of another language (the rounds below say why): in the first try from generation 2 on, in the second from the start.
  • Each generation: 48 states × 8 replies, policy gradient on all replies with nonzero A: LoRA in the first two tries, all weights in the third. Every few generations two blind readers check a sample, so the model cannot win by only sounding like it uses knowledge.

It went the wrong way on every number we care about but one. Progress rose after one generation and then fell (51.5% → 54.7% → 46.2% → 42.2%); replies with no valid act rose from 26 to 117 of 472; and knowledge use fell (27B pass 1: 32.8% → 16.9% of the replies that act; weak uses in the training batches 74 → 52 → 20). Only the other language words fell, once they cost 0.5 (66.7% → 45.6%).

The cause is in the update. The loss sums over tokens, so a reply cut at the 16,384 token limit weighs as much as 25 normal replies. These replies are endless loops: the model recites the list again and again, or counts the letters of one board row over and over. They were 47%, 52% and 65% of the trained tokens in generations 1 to 3, all pushed down, and the push made them more common, not less (15, 26, 44 per batch). Long replies are also where the model states rules, so the push hit knowledge use too.

Is the reward paid for the right thing? Two blind readers read what generation 1 paid (they see the thinking and the act, never the reward or the outcome). Of 40 weak use replies, 44% really state a rule and act on it, and 31% pass the strict standard. Of the 2 strong use replies, 100% pass it. Of 20 replies that made progress but were not paid, 0% use a rule. The readers agree well (kappa 0.96). So the weak bonus is a noisy push in the right direction, and the judge rarely misses a real use. The problem of the first try was the update, not the reward.

Second try, from 17 November

The same reward, from the same round 3 model, with one change in the update: a reply counts as at most 2048 tokens.

Ai = (ri − meanj rj) · min(1, 2048 / Li),   Li = tokens of reply i

A loop still loses, but it counts like one reply. On the third batch of the first try this changes the balance of up and down pushes from 1 : 3.2 to 1 : 1.5. The cost for other language words is on from the first generation.

After 3 generations progress had risen from 51.5% to 68.0%, but knowledge use had not moved (27B pass 1: 32.8%, 33.4%, 32.8%, 35.1%). Split by use, the replies that use knowledge made progress 79% to 86% of the time all along; the gain came from replies that do not (45% → 62%). That is better play without knowledge. So from generation 4 the knowledge bonus is doubled: 1.0 for a weak use and 2.0 for a strong one, so a use is worth as much as progress itself.

With the doubled bonus, use rose for the first time. From generation 3 to generation 7, 27B pass 1 went from 35.1% to 61.2% of the replies that act, and strict use with progress from 4 to 15 replies. Progress rose to 85.6% and other language words fell to 19.3%. Recitation fell from 39.0% to 18.4%; nothing pays for it.

Two blind readers then read the start and generation 7: 2 replies that act per state from each, 236 replies mixed together with no labels. Strict use, weighted per state:

start: 15.3%,   generation 7: 43.4%;   difference +28.1 points, 95% CI [18.9, 37.4];   κ = 0.82

So the rise the 27B sees is real. It is not one template: the real uses cover 42 of the 59 states and 25 statements of K, against 22 and 18 at the start. But it leans on one rule, "The upper-right tile band is a colour selector: a press on a tile loads its colour […]", which makes up 38% of the real uses (15% at the start); 86 of the 104 choose a click and 17 a pour. The same start model read 21.0% when it was read together with generation 2, so two readings of one model can differ by about 6 points.

Third try, from 17 November: all weights

Next: learn only the knowledge, graft it, then RL over several steps

A start that keeps the base model's reasoning

The model after the knowledge SFT did not learn only K. Its rows put the knowledge sentences on top of the base model's own thinking, and that thinking was trained too: the loss on it fell from 0.81 to 0.02, so the model learned its 119 thinks by heart, acts included. Now only the knowledge sentences are learned: the same rows, but the model's own thinking after the sentences stays in each row as context with no loss on it, with a LoRA on the base 9B. The learned change is then grafted back onto the base model in four ways: all of it; the feed-forward layers only; the attention layers only; the largest tenth of each layer's change; or it is added at a fraction of its size (0.2 to 0.65 times). Each version is checked for the knowledge, for how far its thinking moved, and for maths and play, before any RL. Learning only the knowledge broke the model. The full graft holds the knowledge best (loss on the content of K 0.12, against 3.21 for the base model), but its thinking moved far from the base model's (3.14 and 3.70, against 1.16 and 1.30), and it no longer acts: none of its 472 replies on the 59 unseen states closes its thinking; it lists knowledge sentences until the token limit or stops in the middle of the list. MATH-500 falls from 69.0% to 1.2%: its maths replies start well and then repeat one statement. The rows taught that a sentence follows a sentence, and never the step back to reasoning.

Grafting and merging do not save it. Each part of the change holds part of the knowledge and carries part of the damage: the feed-forward layers hold most of the knowledge and half of the change in thinking, the attention layers little of either. On 32 training states the base model acts in 27 of 64 replies; every graft and every scaled merge acts in none, down to 0.2 times the change, which keeps little of the knowledge (2.32) and moves the thinking only a little (1.29 and 1.48). Even that small dose turns the opening of the thinking into a list of statements, and the model never comes back from it. So no point on this curve has both the knowledge and the play.

So the SFT itself was changed: the same rows and the same loss on the knowledge sentences, plus a term that keeps every other token of the reply where the base model has it (the KL divergence from the base model, the same weights with the LoRA switched off). The step back to reasoning after the sentences is then learned from the base model itself. This holds the knowledge as well as the full graft (0.11) while the thinking stays where it was (1.17 and 1.41, against 1.16 and 1.30), and the model still plays: on the 32 training states it acts in 22 of 64 replies (the base model 27), on the 59 unseen states in 187 of 472 (the base model 197), with a little more progress (3.0% of replies, against 2.1%). It now brings up the knowledge by itself in 8% of its replies there; the base model never does. The 27B finds a use of it in 30 of the 187 replies that act, but the strict second reading keeps only 1, and the RL reward would pay none of them. Its replies open with a few knowledge sentences, then go back to reasoning and act.

Maths is what it lost: MATH-500 falls from 69.0% to 53.2%. Of the 120 problems the base model solves and this model does not, 117 end at the token limit: the model finds the answer and then cannot stop (“I will write the response. The reasoning is done.” over and over). The KL term only covered game replies, so nothing held the maths in place. The next try adds one more round with the base model's own maths: 1000 problems of the public MATH training split (none of them in MATH-500) answered by the base model, and the 639 replies that finish within 12288 tokens go into the same KL term, every token, next to half a pass more of the knowledge rows.

That brings most of the maths back: MATH-500 61.2%, and 80 of the 120 lost problems are solved again. The knowledge got stronger in the same round (0.08) and the thinking did not move (1.17 and 1.41). But the model acts less: in 124 of 472 replies on the unseen states, against 187 before this round, because more of its replies put the tool call inside the thinking instead of after it (253 against 154; such a reply makes no move). By the rule fixed before the results, the RL therefore starts from the model before this round, which acts as often as the base model and keeps 53.2% on MATH-500. It started at 21:12 UTC.

To see whether the extra half pass of knowledge caused that, the same round was run again with no loss on the knowledge sentences, only the two KL terms. Maths comes back in full (70.0%, the base model 69.0%), but the knowledge goes with it (1.19 on the content of K, against 0.11 before the round), the 27B finds no use of it at all, and the model acts even less (127 of 472). The base model itself puts the tool call inside its thinking in 226 of 472 replies; the KL-anchored model in 154, the round with the maths in 253, this round in 307. Both rounds that added the maths replies made this worse, so the RL keeps the start the rule picked, and the reward's −1 for a reply that ends the game without an act works on exactly this failure.

RL over several steps

It starts from the KL-anchored model: the one with the maths kept if its MATH-500 is at least 3 points higher and it still acts and holds the knowledge, otherwise the one before it (a rule fixed before the results; it picked the one before it). A training game continues a training state for up to 4 acts, in the same format as the test above. The 27B reads every reply that acts, as before. Reply t of a game earns rt: 1 for a new board, 5 for a cleared level, 2 for a strong and 1 for a weak use (only when the act makes progress), −0.5 for other language words and −1 when it ends the game with no act (a think that never closes, or no tool call). It is scored by its own act and everything after it in the game:

Gt = rt + rt+1 + …At = Gt − mean Gt

where the mean is over the games from the same state that reached reply t. Only the first act of a reply counts, the one the 27B reads. A use counts only when the rule also stands in the reasoning after the opening, not only in a recited list. Training games stop before they reach the board of an unseen state of their own game, and we dropped the 55 training states that start on one (the earlier tries trained on them; boards repeat inside a game).

  • Changed: "Knowledge of the first level" in the prompt was a partial file (89 of 198 claims, without the ACTION3 and ACTION4 inverse), which is why the after swap situation did not improve.
  • Changed: Test states are first exposures, but their boards also appear in training; only the moment is held out.

Setup

Game
cd82 from ARC-AGI-3: a 64 × 64 board, actions ACTION1 to ACTION6 (ACTION6 is a click at x, y) and RESET. We use the first game level only.
Models
Trained: Qwen3.5 9B. Judge: Qwen3.8 27B with thinking off. Readers: Claude agents.
Knowledge
474 claims extracted from recorded play, 391 kept after checks. The list for the first game level has 184 statements; recitation, the judge and the readers all use this list. Routes and coordinate answers were removed at every step.
States
751 states from recorded episodes: 692 for training, 59 for testing. Test states are within the first 12 steps of their episode, the situation is new in that episode, and the prompt has no summary of earlier play.
Progress S
S = P(the step reaches a board not seen before in the episode), from the game engine.
Recite R
R = P(the thinking contains a sentence of K): 8 or more words, 80% of its 4 word pieces. Word for word only.
After the first paragraph
The recited sentence starts after the first blank line of the thinking.
Knowledge loss
Mean token loss on the same 200 knowledge sentences; lower means remembered better. The base 9B: 3.21.
Compute
One H200 GPU.
